# Retatrutide and Body Composition: A Longitudinal Self-Reported Case Study

> **Version:** 0.1.0 (2026-10-29) · **Status:** preliminary, retrospective and ongoing self-report (N=1). **Not peer reviewed; not a clinical trial; not medical advice.** Retatrutide is an investigational compound; neither this report nor its exposure history is a recommendation to use it.

## Abstract
An adult resistance-trained participant reports initiating use of a product sold as retatrutide on **2026-09-14**, with a reported starting and current regimen of **2.5 mg once weekly**. On **2026-10-29**, the participant reported **154.5 kg** body weight and identified the product as **Thera**. Previous clinical body-composition assessments from January–April 2026 predate the reported exposure and are supplied as historical context, **not as treatment baseline measurements**. The participant retrospectively reported reduced appetite, greater energy, less post-cardio sleepiness, and headaches and other symptoms of uncertain temporal association. No standardized post-initiation body-composition assessment, laboratory confirmation of compound identity, systematic symptom diary, or controlled comparison is available. **No causal efficacy or safety conclusions can be drawn.**

## Retrospective self-reported experiences
These observations were recalled during earlier conversations and are **not** daily prospectively scored outcomes:
- **Perceived changes:** diminished hunger, sometimes forgetting to eat; increased energy/positive mood; reduced sleepiness after morning cardio; cardio perceived as easier; perception of a smaller abdomen (not validated by contemporaneous tape measurement).
- **Potential adverse symptoms:** morning headaches; feeling mentally 'spaced out' before lunch; transient stomach discomfort after sweet popcorn. Associations and severity were not clinically adjudicated. Some headaches were reported to subside after eating.
- **Unknown:** actual onset/duration per symptom, standardized appetite scoring, glucose values, lab outcomes, and later persistence or resolution.
- **Confounders:** sustained training and dietary changes; sleep and hydration; supplement or medication exposure (including **BPC-157 beginning 2026-10-28** as independently self-reported in a separate case study). These exposures should be reconciled by date; the fact that BPC-157 started later means it cannot explain September observations.

## Methods and future recording
This is a descriptive, single-participant longitudinal self-observation. The record is organized into:
- `data/body_composition.csv` — historical assessments and weight entries with phase and provenance.
- `data/exposures.csv` — only reported exposure anchors; **not** a manufactured weekly application log.
- `data/symptoms.csv` — retrospectively recalled symptoms, with uncertain dates marked explicitly.
- `data/weekly_tracking_template.csv` — empty template for optional future observations, without encouraging continued exposure.
- `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` — verification needs and limitations.

For future comparisons, record measured body weight under comparable conditions, appetite and well-being, exact symptom dates, treatment changes, and training/diet context; any laboratory or clinical data should be interpreted with a qualified clinician.

## Limitations and safety
This uncontrolled, non-blinded N=1 self-report is vulnerable to recall bias, expectancy effects, measurement variability and major confounding. Different body-composition methods can vary substantially. There is no verified body-fat assessment after initiation; there are no controlled tests and no evidence here for efficacy, dose-response, durability or safety. Retatrutide is investigational and should not be treated as a validated self-medication protocol.
